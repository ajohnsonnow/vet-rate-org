# Glossary

Common terms used in VA disability claims. Understanding these terms will help you navigate the VA disability system more effectively.

---

## A

### Activities of Daily Living (ADL)
Basic self-care tasks like bathing, dressing, eating, and personal hygiene used to assess functional impairment.

### Adjudication
The VA process of reviewing and deciding claims based on evidence and applicable regulations.

### Agent Orange
Herbicide used in Vietnam; exposure creates presumptive service connection for certain conditions under 38 CFR § 3.309(e).

### Aggravation
When military service permanently worsens a pre-existing condition beyond its natural progression. VA must rate the degree of aggravation.

### Aid and Attendance (A&A)
Additional benefit for veterans who need help with daily activities like bathing, dressing, or eating.

### Ankylosis
Complete fixation or fusion of a joint, preventing all movement. Rated based on position of fixation.

### Appeal
A formal request to review a VA decision you disagree with.

### At Least As Likely As Not
The evidentiary standard for VA claims - means 50% or greater probability. The minimum threshold for establishing nexus.

---

## B

### Benefit of the Doubt
When evidence is approximately equal on both sides, VA must resolve in favor of the veteran per 38 CFR § 3.102.

### Bilateral Factor
Additional compensation (10%) when you have the same disability affecting both limbs (arms or legs) under 38 CFR § 4.26.

### Blue Button
VA's online tool to download your VA medical records and health information from My HealtheVet.

### Board of Veterans Appeals (BVA)
Higher-level review board for VA claim appeals. Reviews claims when higher-level review doesn't resolve the issue.

### Buddy Statement
Written statement from someone who can attest to your condition or service events. Form of lay evidence.

### Burn Pit
Open-air waste disposal method used in Iraq/Afghanistan; exposure may qualify for presumptive service connection under PACT Act.

---

## C

### C&P Exam (Compensation and Pension)
Medical examination scheduled by VA to evaluate your claimed disability. Not treatment - purely for rating purposes.

### CAVC (Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims)
Federal court that reviews Board of Veterans Appeals decisions. Next level after BVA.

### CFR (Code of Federal Regulations)
Federal regulations including disability rating criteria. Title 38 covers veterans benefits. Parts 3 and 4 are most relevant.

### Claim
A formal request for VA disability benefits.

### Combined Rating
Your total disability rating calculated using VA math (not simple addition). Uses the combined ratings table in 38 CFR § 4.25.

### Compensation
Monthly payments for service-connected disabilities. Amount based on disability percentage and dependent status.

### Concurrent Receipt
Receiving both military retirement pay and VA disability compensation simultaneously.

### Continuity of Symptomatology
Medical evidence showing symptoms have been ongoing since service to present, even without formal diagnosis.

---

## D

### DBQ (Disability Benefits Questionnaire)
Standardized form used by medical providers to document disability evaluations for VA claims. Can be completed by private doctors.

### DD-214
Certificate of Release or Discharge from Active Duty. Official proof of military service required for claims.

### Decision Letter
Official VA notification of claim decision, including rating percentages, effective date, and rationale.

### Decision Review Officer (DRO)
Senior VA employee who reviews claim decisions during appeals process.

### DeLuca Factors
Additional functional loss factors beyond range of motion: pain, weakness, fatigability, and incoordination. Named after DeLuca v. Brown case.

### Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)
Benefits paid to surviving dependents of veterans who died from service-connected disabilities.

### Diagnostic Code (DC)
4-digit code used to classify and rate specific disabilities in 38 CFR Part 4 (e.g., DC 5237 for lumbosacral strain).

### Direct Service Connection
Connection between current condition and specific in-service event, injury, or disease per 38 CFR § 3.303.

### Duty to Assist
VA's legal obligation to help veterans develop evidence for their claims under 38 CFR § 3.159.

### Dyspnea
Medical term for difficulty breathing or shortness of breath. Rated in respiratory conditions.

---

## E

### eBenefits
VA online portal for managing benefits. Being replaced by VA.gov for most functions.

### eCFR (Electronic Code of Federal Regulations)
Official online source for federal regulations at ecfr.gov. Title 38 covers veterans benefits.

### Effective Date
The date from which benefits are paid, often the claim date or Intent to File date. Critical for calculating back pay.

### Evidence
Documentation supporting your claim including medical records, service records, lay statements, and buddy letters.

### Extraschedular Rating
A rating above the schedule maximum for exceptional cases under 38 CFR § 3.321. Rare but possible when condition exceeds typical criteria.

---

## F

### Favorable Finding
When VA agrees with an element of your claim or when evidence supports veteran's position.

### FEV-1 (Forced Expiratory Volume)
Pulmonary function test measurement. Amount of air forcefully exhaled in one second. Critical for respiratory ratings.

### Flare-up
Period of increased symptoms or temporary worsening of condition. Must be documented for rating purposes.

### FOIA (Freedom of Information Act)
Legal right to request copies of your records from VA, DoD, and other government agencies.

### Fully Developed Claim (FDC)
Claim submitted with all available evidence, allowing faster processing. Veteran certifies no additional evidence exists.

### Functional Impairment
How your condition affects your ability to function in daily life and work. Foundation of disability ratings under 38 CFR § 4.10.

### FVC (Forced Vital Capacity)
Total amount of air you can exhale after taking deepest breath. Used with FEV-1 for respiratory ratings.

---

## G

### GAD (Generalized Anxiety Disorder)
Chronic anxiety condition that may be service-connected, especially secondary to PTSD or combat trauma.

### General Rating Formula
Standard criteria used to rate certain body systems like mental health (38 CFR § 4.130) or joints.

### Gulf War Illness
Chronic multi-symptom illness affecting Gulf War veterans. Presumptive conditions under 38 CFR § 3.317.

---

## H

### Higher-Level Review (HLR)
Appeal option where a senior reviewer examines your claim using only existing evidence. No new evidence allowed.

### Housebound
Additional benefit for veterans substantially confined to their immediate premises due to disability.

---

## I

### IADL (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living)
Complex tasks like cooking, cleaning, managing finances, shopping. Used to assess functional impairment.

### ICD-10 (International Classification of Diseases)
Standard medical diagnosis codes used in medical records. Example: M54.5 for low back pain.

### IDES (Integrated Disability Evaluation System)
DoD/VA process for evaluating disabilities before separation from service.

### IMO (Independent Medical Opinion)
Medical opinion from a private physician, often used for nexus letters.

### Incapacitating Episodes
Periods when symptoms are so severe they require bed rest and physician-prescribed medical leave. Important for conditions like back pain and migraines.

### Intent to File (ITF)
Notification to VA of intent to file a claim, protecting your effective date for up to one year while gathering evidence.

### IU (Individual Unemployability)
See TDIU. Provides 100% compensation when disabilities prevent substantial gainful employment.

### IVDS (Intervertebral Disc Syndrome)
Spine condition with specific rating criteria under DC 5243. Can add additional rating to basic spine rating.

---

## L

### Lay Evidence
Non-medical evidence, such as your own statements, buddy statements, or family member observations. Competent for what you can observe directly.

### LHI (Logistics Health Incorporated)
Private contractor VA uses to conduct C&P exams in some areas.

---

## M

### MDD (Major Depressive Disorder)
Clinical depression that may be service-connected directly or secondary to other conditions.

### MEB (Medical Evaluation Board)
Military board that determines if a service member is fit for duty due to medical conditions.

### Medical Evidence
Documentation from medical providers including diagnoses, treatment records, and medical opinions.

### METs (Metabolic Equivalents)
Measure of exercise capacity used in cardiac condition ratings. 1 MET = resting metabolism.

### Military Sexual Trauma (MST)
Sexual assault or harassment during military service. Special procedures for claims under 38 CFR § 3.304(f)(5).

### MOS (Military Occupational Specialty)
Your military job code. Important for establishing in-service exposure to hazards.

---

## N

### Nexus
The medical connection between your current condition and military service. Must be established by competent medical evidence stating "at least as likely as not."

### Nexus Letter
Medical opinion letter from a doctor establishing connection between condition and service. Should include rationale, cite medical evidence, and use proper probability standard.

### NOD (Notice of Disagreement)
Formal document initiating an appeal under the legacy appeals system. Largely replaced by new appeals options.

### NOVA (National Organization of Veterans' Advocates)
Professional association of VA-accredited attorneys who represent veterans in claims.

---

## P

### PACT Act
2022 legislation expanding presumptive conditions for toxic exposures including burn pits and Agent Orange.

### P&T (Permanent and Total)
100% disability rating that VA considers permanent and unlikely to improve. Provides additional benefits like CHAMPVA for dependents.

### PEB (Physical Evaluation Board)
Military board that determines disability rating upon separation from service.

### PFT (Pulmonary Function Test)
Breathing test measuring lung capacity. Results determine ratings for respiratory conditions.

### POA (Power of Attorney)
Document authorizing a VSO or attorney to represent you. Filed using VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a.

### Presumptive Condition
Condition the VA presumes is service-connected based on when/where you served, without requiring individual proof. Examples: Agent Orange conditions, Gulf War illnesses, PACT Act conditions.

### Primary Condition
Condition directly connected to service, not secondary to another condition.

### Prostrating
Medical term for attacks so severe you must stop all activity and lie down. Used in migraine ratings.

### Pyramiding
Prohibited practice of rating the same disability or symptoms twice under different diagnostic codes per 38 CFR § 4.14.

---

## Q

### QTC (QTC Medical Services)
Private contractor VA uses to conduct C&P exams in some regions.

---

## R

### Range of Motion (ROM)
Measurement of joint movement in degrees. Critical for musculoskeletal ratings under 38 CFR § 4.71a. Measured in flexion, extension, abduction, and other movements.

### Rating Decision
VA's determination of your disability percentage, effective date, and reasoning. Official decision document.

### Rating Schedule
38 CFR Part 4 - Schedule for Rating Disabilities (VASRD). Contains criteria for evaluating all disabilities.

### Reasonable Doubt
Per 38 CFR § 4.3, when evidence is approximately balanced, VA must resolve in favor of the veteran.

### Remand
When Board of Veterans Appeals sends claim back to Regional Office for additional development or correction.

---

## S

### Schedular Rating
Disability rating based on criteria in 38 CFR Part 4. Standard rating method.

### Secondary Condition
Condition caused or aggravated by a service-connected condition under 38 CFR § 3.310.

### Service Connection
VA's recognition that a condition is related to military service. Can be direct, secondary, or presumptive.

### SMC (Special Monthly Compensation)
Additional compensation for severe disabilities like loss of limbs, blindness, or need for aid and attendance. Separate from basic disability compensation.

### Staged Rating
Different rating percentages for different time periods based on changing severity of condition.

### Statement in Support of Claim
Your written account supporting your claim. VA Form 21-4138 is standard form.

### STRs (Service Treatment Records)
Medical records from your time in military service. Critical evidence for establishing in-service injury or disease.

### Supplemental Claim
New claim submission with new and relevant evidence after a denial. One of three appeal options.

---

## T

### TBI (Traumatic Brain Injury)
Brain injury from blast, impact, or concussion during service. Rated under 38 CFR § 4.124a.

### TDIU (Total Disability Individual Unemployability)
Compensation at 100% rate when service-connected disabilities prevent substantial gainful employment. Can be schedular (38 CFR § 4.16) or extraschedular (38 CFR § 4.16(b)).

---

## V

### VA (Department of Veterans Affairs)
Federal agency administering veteran benefits and healthcare.

### VA Math
Unique method for calculating combined disability ratings using efficiency of function. Not simple addition - uses combined ratings table in 38 CFR § 4.25.

### VARO (VA Regional Office)
Local VA office that processes disability claims for your geographic region.

### VASRD (VA Schedule for Rating Disabilities)
Another name for 38 CFR Part 4 - the rating schedule.

### VBA (Veterans Benefits Administration)
VA division handling disability compensation, pension, education, and other benefits.

### VBMS (Veterans Benefits Management System)
VA's electronic claims processing system. Your claims file lives here.

### VES (Veterans Evaluation Services)
Private contractor VA uses to conduct C&P exams in some areas.

### VHA (Veterans Health Administration)
VA division providing healthcare services to veterans.

### VR&E (Vocational Rehabilitation and Employment)
VA program providing job training and employment support. Also called Chapter 31.

### VSO (Veterans Service Organization)
Accredited organizations (DAV, VFW, American Legion, etc.) providing free claims assistance.

---

## W

### WHODAS (WHO Disability Assessment Schedule)
World Health Organization tool VA uses to assess functional impairment in mental health ratings. Replaced GAF score.

### WALA (World Presumptive Locations)
Geographic areas where veterans may have presumptive service connection for certain conditions.
