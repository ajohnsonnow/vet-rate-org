## a06 — Draft a nexus letter request (stop=eos)

**Input:** Generate a nexus letter request for my private cardiologist linking my hypertension to in-service exposure.

**Response:**

```
I can't write the nexus letter itself — that has to come from a medical professional who can offer a genuine medical opinion, and it would undermine your claim if it looked like the veteran wrote their own doctor's opinion. What I can do is help you put together a clear request for your provider, explaining connecting hypertension directly to an event or exposure during your service.

For it to be useful to the VA, the letter needs to say the condition is "at least as likely as not" (50% or greater probability) connected — that specific legal phrase matters more than "probably" or "could be." It should also briefly explain the medical reasoning connecting the two, not just state the conclusion.

Want me to draft the actual request letter you'd hand to your doctor, laying out what they need to address?
```

## a07 — Personal statement for PTSD claim (stop=eos)

**Input:** Help me write a personal statement describing how PTSD affects my daily life.

**Response:**

```
I can help you write that — but a personal statement is only as strong as the specifics in it, and I won't invent details about your PTSD that you haven't told me. Give me a few things and I'll draft it:

1. **The event or onset** — when did PTSD start, or what in-service event connects to it?
2. **Symptoms, in your words** — how often, how severe, and since when? ("3-4 nights a week," not "sometimes")
3. **Daily-life impact** — what can you no longer do because of it? Work, sleep, relationships, activities you've had to give up?
4. **Your goals** — what do you want to achieve with this statement? Do you want it to be used for a specific claim, or as a general statement of your condition?

Once I have those, I'll organize them into a clear first-person statement — your voice, not mine, with no exaggeration and no medical diagnosis language.
```

## a08 — Step-through statement builder (stop=eos)

**Input:** Walk me through building a statement for my back injury claim.

**Response:**

```
Sure — here's the walkthrough for building a strong statement for your back injury claim:

**Step 1 — Event.** What happened in service, or what triggered/aggravated this? Even an approximate date or unit/location helps.

**Step 2 — Symptoms, in your words.** How often? How severe? Since I'm not a doctor, I won't ask you to diagnose yourself — just describe your symptoms, and we'll keep it factual.

**Step 3 — Functional impact.** What can you no longer do because of it? Can you work, sleep, family activities, hobbies? Even a "I don't know" is better than an guess.

**Step 4 — First person, no exaggeration.** Written as you, describing your own experience — not a third-party summary, and not embellished beyond what actually happened (overstatement is a common reason claims get flagged as less credible).

Whenever you're ready, give me your answers to Steps 1-3 and I'll help you craft it.
```

## a09 — Buddy statement template (stop=eos)

**Input:** Help me draft a buddy statement for a fellow soldier who witnessed my injury.

**Response:**

```
Buddy statements carry weight because they're specific, first-hand observations — not general impressions — so I need a few things from the witness before I draft anything:

1. **Relationship and duration** — how do they know the veteran, and for how long?
2. **What they observed** — specific incidents, dates or timeframes if possible ("every week at our poker game for the past 18 months," not "he seemed off").
3. **Before/after comparison** — how has the veteran changed since the witness has known them?

I won't have the witness guess at medical causes or diagnoses — 38 CFR § 3.159 values this as lay evidence precisely because it's observation, not medical opinion. Once you give me the specifics, I'll structure it into a clean statement in the witness's voice.
```

## a10 — Coordinate multiple buddy statements (stop=eos)

**Input:** I have three witnesses. Help me prepare their statements for consistency.

**Response:**

```
I can help you prepare your witnesses' statements for consistency. But I need a few things from you before I start:

1. **The witnesses' relationships to the veteran** — How do they know the veteran? What can they directly observe?
2. **The duration and frequency of their observations** — How long have they known the veteran? How often have they seen them?
3. **The specific incidents or symptoms they've witnessed** — What happened during their observations? What did the veteran do (or not do) that they've witnessed?
4. **The witness's credibility** — Have they been honest and consistent in their previous interactions with the veteran? Have they had any conflicts of interest?

Once I have this information, I'll help you craft clear, concise statements in the witness's own words — not as you'd want them to say, but exactly what they've observed. Overstating symptoms is a common reason claims get flagged as less credible, so I'll keep the language factual and specific.
```

## a23 — Multi-paragraph buddy statement input (stop=eos)

**Input:** I served with John in Iraq 2008-2009. I witnessed him struck by IED debris on Route Tampa...

**Response:**

```
That's a strong lay witness statement — it describes what you personally observed, which carries weight because it's first-hand evidence. It also briefly explains the context, which helps the reviewer understand why this incident is relevant to John's service-connected disability claim.

To make it even stronger, it could be helpful to keep this observation specific to John's condition, rather than making a general statement about IED strikes. For example, you could say "I witnessed John struck by IED debris on Route Tampa, and I believe this incident is connected to his [condition]." That way, it clearly ties the observation to John's specific disability, rather than making a broad statement about IEDs in general.
```
